Evaluating Family Business & Startup Equity During Divorce

Contributed by Adv. Vikramaditya Rao (Delhi High Court)

Published on January 11, 2026 ← Back to Blog

Dividing traditional assets like real estate is relatively straightforward. However, modern divorces frequently involve startup stock options, private company equity, and family business holdings.

The Challenge of Private Equity

Unlike public shares, private startup equity has no active marketplace to establish its immediate cash value. If a spouse holds ESOPs (Employee Stock Ownership Plans) or founder shares, determining their fair value requires sophisticated forensic accounting.

Valuation Methodologies

Indian courts consider multiple valuation frameworks depending on the stage of the business:

  • Net Asset Value (NAV): Best suited for asset-heavy family businesses.
  • Discounted Cash Flow (DCF): Focuses on projected future earnings, common for established tech firms.
  • Recent Funding Rounds: Evaluates value based on institutional investments.

Structuring the Settlement

To prevent operational disruption to the company, founder shares are rarely split in half. Instead, settlements are structured through:

  • Cash Out option: One spouse buys out the other's imputed equity share.
  • Structured Offsets: Offsetting the equity value by transferring other marital assets (such as real estate or retirement funds) to the non-owning spouse.
  • Deferred Payouts: Structuring payments to align with future liquidity events (e.g. an IPO or acquisition).

Conclusion

Handling business equity during divorce requires a collaborative effort between legal counsel and financial evaluators. Retaining expert forensic assistance early in the process ensures an equitable, stable division.
